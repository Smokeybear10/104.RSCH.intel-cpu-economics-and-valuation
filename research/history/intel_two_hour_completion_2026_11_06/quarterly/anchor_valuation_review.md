# What the favorable anchor test changes

The forecast cuts are not a disproof of the original long. Reasonable favorable interpretations of Q4 guidance almost restore the original 2027 revenue targets. They do not, by themselves, restore the original investment return.

All outcomes below use the same $52.25 reference price and December 2030 horizon. They are unweighted scenarios, not confidence intervals.

| Operating and retention assumptions | 2030 value | Annual price return |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selected quarterly path, YTD CPU margin, normalized later growth | $53.43 | 0.54% |
| Favorable Q4 anchors, same later assumptions | $56.89 | 2.07% |
| Favorable anchors plus old post-2030 CPU growth | $62.41 | 4.37% |
| Above plus pooled Q2/Q3 CPU margin | $65.23 | 5.49% |
| Above plus old 2028–30 CPU growth from the favorable 2027 base | $73.56 | 8.58% |
| Exact old 2027–30 CPU/client path, pooled margin and old later growth | $73.28 | 8.48% |

The last two controls keep the revised Other DCAI growth and selected Foundry margin treatment. They are not recreations of every original bullish assumption. Restoring additional favorable assumptions would need to be identified separately.

The joint favorable anchor uses a 21% sequential CCPG decline, 17% DCAI growth and 25% Other DCAI growth. Q4 consolidated revenue becomes $15.887 billion, inside the $15–16 billion company range. Under the specified 50% group profit response, 15% tax and 10% working-capital convention, Q4 operating cash rises $152.8 million. This enters opening 2027 cash once. The 2027 working-capital bridge subtracts the prior-year sales change, preventing a second charge for the same Q4 balance.

The favorable anchor produces 2027 CPU sales of $31.739 billion and CCPG sales of $28.390 billion, compared with the old $31.475 billion and $28.554 billion. It produces $72.423 billion consolidated sales, versus $69.845 billion selected. Therefore the chosen lower 2027 forecast is one reasonable planning case, not uniquely established by the public evidence.

The primary unresolved issue is how much growth and profit survive after the first capacity ramp. In the selected case, approximately 93% of operating value at the 2026 valuation date comes from cash flows after 2030. That is broader than the perpetuity alone. Delivering a good 2027 would not validate that later value.

The favorable tests do not establish a high-confidence long or short. They show the difference between a defensible operating thesis and a demonstrated price edge. A strong defense should state the growth, realized pricing, cost and capital conditions required for each outcome, then identify which next disclosures would change the forecast.
